## Kazan and Kazakhstan: similar names, unrelated markets

Kazan is in Russia. The provider page that actually sells there describes the product as hosting "aimed at the audience of Tatarstan and surrounding regions," and a second listing calls Kazan "one of Russia's most important technological and economic centers." Kazakhstan's VPS market is a separate thing entirely, with data centers in Almaty and Astana and a different set of users on the other end of the wire.

That difference changes three practical things:

**Latency target.** A server in Almaty is in a different country and a different network from your users in Tatarstan. Any ping number quoted for Almaty tells you nothing about response times in Kazan. If your traffic is Russian, a Kazakhstan node is answering the wrong question.

**Where the data sits.** Russia and Kazakhstan have separate data regulations. If a client or an internal policy requires data to stay inside Russia, a Kazakhstan instance fails that requirement no matter how fast it is.

**Who supports you.** The Kazan provider makes a point of same-time-zone support, which shortens the loop when something breaks at 2 a.m. That argument only holds if the support desk is actually in your time zone.

One claim on the Kazan page deserves a raised eyebrow: that a local node improves local SEO indicators. Server location is a weak signal at best. The latency argument is the one with real weight behind it, and it is enough on its own.

## Picking a plan by workload

**One low-traffic site, a landing page, a small API.** 1 vCPU and 1 GB is enough. Take DLine Combo 1, JustHost Sirius or LightNode Start. Do not buy 4 GB because the price column looked more comfortable.

**WordPress with a cache plugin, two or three Docker containers, a small database.** 4 GB is the practical floor. DLine Combo 2, JustHost Vega, LightNode Agency. Below 4 GB, MySQL and a web server start competing for the same memory and you will be tuning swap instead of shipping.

**A game server, streaming, or anything with real concurrent connections.** Look at the uplink, not the RAM. DLine is unusually explicit here, splitting 100 / 300 / 500 Mbit/s across its range, and Combo 3 and above all get 500 Mbit/s. JustHost runs 300 Mbit/s on the small tiers up to 750 Mbit/s on the larger ones, which puts it ahead of the Kazan provider's entry tier but behind it in the mid range. On a fair-share port you are also sharing with neighbours, so a dedicated gigabit line is a separate purchase.

**Crawling, scraping, proxy nodes.** The IPv6-only line at $1.16 to a few dollars a month is the cheapest way to get many IPs. Verify your target sites accept IPv6 first, because a chunk of Russian retail and banking infrastructure still does not.

**Multiple servers that talk to each other.** DLine supports VLAN grouping, which is the feature you want here. Running an app server and a database on public interfaces is how you end up firewalling in a hurry.

## What to confirm on the order page

None of the following is answered in the public pages reviewed for this article. All of it changes the total cost or whether the server is usable at all.

- **The actual Kazan data center.** City-level marketing is not the same as a named facility. Ask which building, then check that it has generator backup.
- **Whether DDoS protection is included and how large the scrubbing capacity is.** "Protected" without a number is a marketing word.
- **The port ceiling and whether it is dedicated or fair-share.** A 500 Mbit/s fair-share port is not a 500 Mbit/s guarantee.
- **Billing period and renewal price.** JustHost's 20% discount is tied to a 12-month prepay. The monthly rate is what you pay if you do not commit, and it is the number to use when comparing against month-to-month providers.
- **Refund window or trial.** Not stated in any of the material reviewed here. Confirm before paying for a year.
- **Payment methods.** A Russian project often needs Russian payment rails, and an Asian-facing brand may route through different processors.
- **IPv4 inclusion.** The cheap IPv6-only tiers will not reach a payment gateway that only speaks v4.
- **Where the data physically lives.** If your client requires Russian territory, get that in writing rather than inferring it from a city name.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between a Kazan VPS and a Kazakhstan VPS?**
Country, jurisdiction, network and audience. Kazan is in Russia, in Tatarstan. Kazakhstan's data centers are in Almaty and Astana. A Kazakhstan server serves Kazakh and Central Asian users well and does nothing for latency in Tatarstan.

**Who actually needs a Kazan node?**
Anyone whose users, customers or clients are concentrated in Tatarstan and the surrounding Volga region, or who has a contractual reason to keep data in Russia. If your audience is in Moscow, a Moscow node is the better answer. If it is in Central Asia, look at the Kazakhstan table above.

**Is 1 GB of RAM enough?**
For a static site, a small API or a single low-traffic WordPress install with caching, yes. For anything running a database alongside the web server, no. Go to 4 GB.

**Why do some Kazan pages show no prices at all?**
The DLine page has six configurations and a pricing heading with nothing under it. Whether that is a deliberate quote-on-request model or a page that renders prices dynamically, the effect is the same: no verifiable number exists. Do not accept a figure from anyone who cannot point to a live page.
